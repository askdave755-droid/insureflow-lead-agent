## 1. The 4-minute demo script (word for word)

Open the live agent (`index.html`) in front of them. **Don't explain the technology.** Let them watch.

| Clock | You do | You say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Click **"Run full demo lead"** | "I'm going to play a customer. Watch what happens on the right-hand side." |
| 0:20 | Let the trucking lead run | "Twelve power units, four states, renewing in 90 days. See the score climbing as it learns more." |
| 1:10 | Point at the **Lead** tab | "82 out of 100. And it shows you *why* — that's the part that matters, because you can argue with it." |
| 1:30 | Switch to **CRM payload** | "That's the record at the end. Name, units, renewal, loss history, and the consent trail — straight into your CRM. Nobody typed it." |
| 2:00 | Switch to **Tool log** | "Every action it took, timestamped. If a customer ever says 'your bot promised me a price,' you have the receipt that says it didn't." |
| 2:20 | **The moment that sells it** — type: *"just give me a ballpark price"* | Let them watch it refuse, warmly, and route to a human. **Then stop talking for three seconds.** This is the whole pitch. |
| 2:50 | Type: *"my policy got non-renewed last week"* | "See it jump — that's not a lead anymore, that's a fire. It skips qualification and pages someone." |
| 3:15 | Type: *"stop texting me"* | "And it's gone. Permanently suppressed. That's your CASL and TCPA exposure handled before it becomes a problem." |
| 3:30 | Close | "Four minutes, and you saw it qualify, refuse, escalate, and shut itself off. Which of those would you want first?" |

**Rule:** never talk over the agent while it's typing. Silence is the demo. If you narrate the whole thing they watch you, not the product.

## 3. The demo link page (what the calendar invite points to)

One line at the top, three sentences max, then the agent:

> "This is a live demo agent. Give it a fake name — don't enter real customer data. Try asking it for a price, and try telling it your policy was cancelled. Those two answers are the whole product."

Naming the two "tricks" **first** is what makes them convincing. A prospect who finds the guardrails themselves trusts them; a prospect who feels tricked into finding them doesn't.

## 4. Who to call first (ranked by pain, not by size)

**Tier A — call this week (you have an unfair angle)**
1. **Commercial insurance agencies** — including your own. You are the reference customer. You can say "I ran it in my book for two weeks and here's the number" while it's still true.
2. **Independent brokerages, 3–25 producers** — big enough to have lead leakage, small enough to decide in one meeting.
3. **Trucking companies** running 10–100 units — they field constant driver and shipper inquiries and live on the phone.

**Tier B — next**
4. Real estate teams and property managers (rental inquiries are exactly this problem)
5. HVAC, plumbing, roofing, landscaping, snow removal — seasonal spikes break their intake every year
6. Med spas, dental, legal intake, private clinics — high-value, appointment-driven, very receptive

**Disqualify fast:**
- No inbound inquiry flow at all (nothing to fix)
- Fewer than ~10 leads/month (can't justify the fee yet — take them later)
- Anyone who wants "AI to replace staff" — that's a different, worse project, and it will end badly

**The first five calls, in order:** your own agency → the broker you respect most → a trucking client you already insure → the contractor who does your shop's work → a realtor who's complained to you about lead response.

## 5. Pricing rationale (so you can defend the number)

The monthly fee has to be explainable in one sentence to someone who doesn't care about AI:

| Their business | Value of one won lead | Agent needs to win |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial insurance agency | $1,500–$4,000 yr-1 commission | 1 policy every 2–3 months |
| Trucking, 10–100 units | $2,000–$6,000 policy | ~1 account per quarter |
| Real estate brokerage | 1 listing agreement | 1 listing per quarter |
| HVAC / trades | $500–$15,000 job | 1 extra job per month |

If you can't say the payback in one sentence, your price is wrong — not your pitch.

**The three-price move:** always present Pilot, Standard, and Performance. The Performance tier isn't for most buyers — it's there to make Standard look like the safe middle *and* to prove you believe in the outcome. Some of your best customers will pick it.

## 6. What NOT to sell (protects you)

- **Don't sell "AI"** to anyone who asks what model it uses. Answer once, honestly, then bring it back to leakage and booked meetings.
- **Don't promise specific lead volume.** You control response time, not demand.
- **Don't let the agent touch anything that could be construed as advice, quoting, or binding.** That's an E&O problem, not a feature gap. `agent-instructions.md` rules R2–R4 exist for this reason — never negotiate them away to win a deal.
- **Don't sell a lead *list* business by accident.** If a customer wants outbound cold outreach at scale, that's a different compliance conversation (CASL/TCPA/DNC). Say no until you've done the homework.
