# The Sales Voice — exact words for selling the agent
**Purpose:** this is the script for the *other* conversation — the one where you sell the agent. Read it out loud once before your first call. It should feel like a broker, not a software vendor.

---

## 0. The posture

You are not selling AI. Nobody has a budget line called AI. You are selling **"the leads you already pay for stop leaking"** — a number on a spreadsheet they already stare at.

Three sentences to internalise:

> "You're paying for leads. Some of them call after hours and nobody answers. This fixes that — it answers in five seconds, qualifies, books the meeting, and writes it up in your CRM. If it doesn't book you meetings, you cancel."

**Never open with:** "I build AI agents." Most business owners hear "chatbot" and think of a bad experience they've had.

---

## 1. The opener (warm — referral, LinkedIn, or existing contact)

> "Hey [name], I'm not going to pitch you on a website — I want to ask about your intake. When a new inquiry comes in at 8pm on a Friday, what happens to it?"

Then **shut up.** Let them answer. The answer is almost always: *"It goes to voicemail"* / *"I see it Monday"* / *"It goes to my assistant"* / *"Honestly, some slip."*

**The follow-up — the only question that matters:**

> "What's one of those worth to you, if it turns into a policy?"

For a trucking account, that number is often $1,500–$4,000 in year-one commission. For a real estate brokerage, it's a listing agreement. Once they say the number out loud, the rest of the call is arithmetic.

---

## 2. The three diagnostic questions

Ask all three. Write the answers down. These become your demo script.

1. **"How many inquiries a month do you get — quote forms, calls, DMs — and how many would you say get a real response within an hour?"**
   *(The gap between those numbers is your entire pitch.)*
2. **"Who answers when nobody's there — and what do they ask a new lead?"**
   *(Ten different staff asking ten different questions is your second sell: consistency.)*
3. **"Where does that info end up — is it in your CRM, or in someone's head and a text thread?"**
   *(If the answer is 'texts', the CRM piece justifies half the price on its own.)*

**Then reflect it back, verbatim.** This is the whole close:

> "So: roughly 40 inquiries a month, maybe half get a same-day response, and the details live in Marcus's phone. Does that sound about right?"

When they say yes, they've just diagnosed themselves. You're now the person who agreed with them.

---

## 3. The offer (say it in this order)

1. **What it is:** "It's a trained assistant that sits on your site. It knows your business, it asks your qualifying questions, and it books."
2. **The boundary — say this out loud, it *builds* trust:** "It will never quote a price. It's not licensed and it doesn't pretend to be — it says 'a producer will confirm that.' That's deliberate: it protects you."
3. **The proof:** "Let me have you talk to mine. I'll send a link. Ask it for a price and watch what it does."
4. **The pilot:** "Two weeks. If it doesn't book you meetings, you don't continue."
5. **The price — one sentence, then silence.**

---

## 4. The offer table (starting points — validate in your market)

| Tier | Setup | Monthly | What's included |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pilot** | $2,500 | $750/mo | One channel (web chat), your qualifying questions, your knowledge base, CRM/webhook, meeting booking, 2-week tuning window |
| **Standard** | $5,000 | $1,500/mo | Everything + SMS/email follow-up sequence, monthly scorecard, quarterly re-tuning, 2 languages, calendar + CRM two-way sync |
| **Performance** | $1,500 | $75–$150 per *qualified* lead | For clients who hate retainers — and the test of whether *you* believe in the thing |
| **Multi-site / franchise** | $12,000+ | $2,500+/mo | Multiple locations, role-based routing, SLA reporting |

**Say this alongside the number, never after:**

> "It answers in five seconds, at 9pm, on a Sunday. Two extra policies a month covers it several times over. If it doesn't, you're out two weeks — you keep the setup work either way."

**Never discount the setup fee.** Discount the *pilot length*. A discount tells them the work wasn't worth the number.

---

## 5. Objections — the four you will actually get

**"We already have a chatbot on the website."**
> "Does it book meetings, or does it answer FAQs? Most of what I see is a FAQ widget that ends the conversation. I'd ask yours the three questions you just asked me and see what happens. If it does the job, you don't need me — genuinely."

**"I don't want a bot talking to my customers."**
> "Then it probably shouldn't talk to all of them. The one I'd turn it on for is the person who fills out your form at 9pm and goes to bed. Right now nobody talks to them. I'm not replacing your people — I'm replacing the voicemail they never hear."

**"How much?"** *(before the demo)*
> "Two and a half to start, seven-fifty a month for the pilot. But don't take my word for the value — talk to the demo first, two minutes. If it's clumsy, I'd rather you tell me now than after you've paid me."

**"Let me think about it."**
> "Fair. Can I ask what you'd need to see to say yes — because if it's 'two policies a month,' I'll put that in writing as the success measure, and if I miss it you don't pay the monthly."
>
> Then send the demo link within the hour. Not next week. That hour is the difference.

---

## 6. The close

> "Here's what I'd propose. I take two weeks, I build it around your questions and your answers, you get access Friday and we run it live for fourteen days. If it books meetings, we go monthly. If it doesn't, you tell me and we stop. When can I start — Monday or Thursday?"

Two options, both a yes. Standard, and it works.

---

## 7. Non-negotiables (write these into every contract)

- **Monthly, not annual**, for the first two quarters. Trust is earned in months.
- **Success measure written down** — *"N qualified conversations and M booked meetings per month."*
- **Their data stays theirs** — say this unprompted, put it in writing.
- **Human always reachable** — the agent hands off; it never becomes the only door.
- **They own the prompts and knowledge base** if they leave. Yes, really. It costs you nothing and it closes deals.

---

## 8. What to do after every call

Log three things in one line each: **what they said their leak was · what a lead is worth to them · the date you sent the demo link.** Then follow up on day 2, day 5, day 12 — the same cadence your agent runs. Practice what you sell.
